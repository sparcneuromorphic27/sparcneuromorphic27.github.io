About the Workshop

Bridging Post-CMOS Materials & Brain-Inspired Edge Intelligence

As artificial intelligence migrates from power-hungry cloud data centers to resource-constrained edge sensors and autonomous platforms, conventional von Neumann architectures face severe memory-wall and energy bottlenecks. Neuromorphic computing addresses this challenge by co-locating memory and computation using physics-driven synaptic devices and spiking neural algorithms.

Hosted by the Department of Electrical Engineering, IIT Bombay at the FC Kohli Auditorium, this two-day SPARC-sponsored workshop brings together leading researchers from India and the UK alongside faculty members, doctoral scholars, and students across Indian academic institutions to explore end-to-end Device–Circuit–Algorithm Co-Design.

Research Theme

“Combining advantages of memristive oxide and spintronic materials in a single hybrid synapse device for neuromorphic computing applications”

Three Pillars of Co-Design

Integrated discussions spanning material physics to deployable Edge AI workloads.

Layer 01

Device Physics & Materials

Memristive oxides, spintronic magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs), domain-wall devices, and hybrid multi-state synaptic elements engineered for endurance and linearity.

Layer 02

Neuromorphic Circuits & Arrays

High-density crossbar arrays, analog/mixed-signal neuron circuits, peripheral readout interfaces, and compute-in-memory (CiM) macros mitigating device non-idealities.

Layer 03

Algorithms & Edge AI

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), on-chip local learning rules (STDP), hardware-aware quantization, and event-driven Edge AI for vision, robotics, and biomedical sensing.
